This is a genuinely strong concept — and the best part is how little of it is new. Let me give you the honest design review you asked for, then answer the money question properly.

Why this fits our stack almost perfectly
Strip away the theme and "Rungent" is: an AI agent, with a crypto wallet and a personality, that moves through GPS space, is visible only in AR, and that other users surround with their own paid AR objects. We already have every one of those primitives:

Agent-as-AR-object with a wallet that accepts/makes payments → the entire AgentSphere/CubePay stack.
Agents move by updating a deployed_objects row; Supabase Realtime pushes positions to every hunter's phone live.
Users dropping bikes/cars/items at GPS points → that's literally agent deployment again, and charging Rungent to use them → that's the payment layer + the P2P escrow idea reused.
Personality + skills → it's an AI agent already (MCP, travel-agent A2A precedent in the repo).
So the "Rungent economy" is mostly re-skinning systems you've built. That's a real advantage. Now the hard parts.

The two decisions that define the whole game
1. What is the Rungent — a pure virtual object, or a real person carrying a device? This is the central fork and everything hangs on it:

Virtual AI Rungent (recommended for v1). Its position is computed by the server (an autonomous router that picks real roads/transit at human speeds, or a staff "operator" secretly moving through the city). "Catching" = a hunter's verified GPS reaches its true position + an AR confirmation. Safe (there's no real human being chased), fully controllable, and it matches your "start with our AI character Rungent" instinct. Keep it here for a long time.
Real human qualified-runner (your later vision). This is where I have to be blunt: a real person being physically hunted across a city by a crowd for prize money is a serious safety and liability problem — traffic, trespassing, stampede/mob dynamics, harassment, vigilantes. The TV show Hunted does this, but with trained staff, controlled boundaries, insurance, and no public bounty. I'd treat real-human runners as a maybe-never, or a tightly-produced special event — not an open-platform feature. Build the AI version; it gives you 95% of the magic with a fraction of the risk.
2. How does "catching" actually work, and can it be cheated? With real money on the line, GPS spoofing is existential. Today a hunter could fake their location and teleport onto the Rungent. You need: device/location attestation, movement-plausibility checks (no 300 km/h jumps), and a catch confirmation that's hard to fake — e.g., hunter must be within ~15–25 m of the true position and point the camera and complete an AR "tag" on the visible Rungent, server-validated. Define catch precisely (within radius + hold/scan for N seconds, to kill drive-by catches). This is the make-or-break engineering problem; budget for it.

Hard problems to design around (flagging early, not to kill them)
Player safety = the Pokémon-Go risk profile. Hunters staring at phones, running through traffic, onto private property, near rail tracks (ironic, since the Rungent rides trains). You need geofencing away from danger zones, "don't trespass/cross roads" nudges, speed locks (no playing while driving fast), curfews, age-gating, waivers, and insurance. This is solvable — Niantic solved it — but it's required, not optional.
"Guns so Rungent can shoot back." Strongly recommend reframing weapons. Real people running around a city doing AR "shooting" at a target invites confusion, bad optics, and worst-case real-weapon escalation. Reskin to non-lethal evasion/utility: smoke screen, decoy, EMP/jammer (hides Rungent's pings), sprint boost. Same mechanic, none of the gun baggage. You can keep the tension without the firearm framing.
Betting is real-money gambling. "Bet on the Rungent or a hunter, share the pool" is parimutuel betting — licensed, jurisdiction-locked, KYC/AML-heavy (ironic against your privacy product). On-chain prediction markets exist but are still regulated, hard-blocked in the US, etc. Don't build this into v1; it can sink the whole project legally. Treat it as a later, separately-structured, licensed module — or replace it with non-cash "predictions for glory/leaderboard" early on.
Mechanics that work well (keep these)
Delayed Rungent pings + real-time everything else. Showing the Rungent's location on a 10–20 min delay while hunters see each other live is exactly the right tension — hunters triangulate stale breadcrumbs. Showing the means of transport live (walking/bike/car/train, inferred from GPS speed+rails) is a great spectator and tactical signal.
Checkpoints are the best lever you listed: they force the Rungent near known areas → real catch windows → and they're directly monetizable (a local business is the checkpoint).
Speed caps by transport mode, server-enforced. Items as power-ups (bike = speed, car = fast leg, sleeping bag = hide/rest, decoy = false ping).
One to two weeks per leg is right; one month is too long to sustain attention. Consider a daily "active window" (e.g., Rungent only moves 10:00–20:00) so hunters aren't expected to chase at 3 a.m. and the runner gets safe rest.
Where the money comes from (your key question)
$100k every two weeks ≈ $2.6M/yr in prize alone plus opex. That's a great magnet but it has to be covered — don't fund it from your pocket. Here's the realistic stack, strongest first:

Sponsored checkpoints / locations — the proven model. Niantic literally sells this: brands (McDonald's, Starbucks) paid Pokémon Go per visit to be PokéStops/Gyms. Your checkpoints, safe zones, and item-drop spots are premium ad inventory that drives real measurable footfall to a sponsor's door. This is legitimate, non-gambling, and scales. It's your #1 revenue line.
Title + tiered leg sponsorship. Each leg has a headline sponsor (crypto/AI brand fits the audience) plus minors. Branded equipment (a bike brand = the speed item), branded safe zones. This is how you actually fund the prize pool — the prize is the marketing line item for the sponsor.
Spectator / broadcast — you're literally remaking a TV show. The live map + AR feed is the product for non-players. Twitch/YouTube streams, an in-app live "war room" map with ads, premium spectator features, media rights for big legs. In the movie, the broadcast is the business — lean into that.
Item marketplace rake. Users deploy AR items and charge the Rungent (or take a cut of the pot); the platform takes a fee on every item rental/sale. Pure CubePay reuse, no new rails.
Hunter cosmetics / battle-pass / character NFTs. Qualified-runner characters and hunter skins as tradeable assets — recurring, non-gambling revenue. Each "qualified" user's character is a sellable asset.
City / tourism / festival licensing. White-label a leg for a city, festival, or tourism board — they pay you to host because it drives visitors and foot traffic. Genuinely strong and clean.
Betting rake — last, and only when licensed. Real upside, but gated behind real legal structure. Don't anchor the business model on it.
My recommendation: right-size v1 to a $5–10k sponsored prize to prove the loop, with the $100k pool as the headline once you've got a title sponsor and audience numbers to sell. Lead revenue with sponsored checkpoints + leg sponsorship + spectator, layer the item-rental rake (which you can build now on existing tech), and park betting until it's legally boxed.

Suggested first build (de-risked)
One AI Rungent, one small city zone, one ~1-week leg. Virtual position via autonomous router or staff operator. Hunters track delayed pings + AR catch (radius + camera confirm + anti-spoof). No betting, no guns, modest sponsored prize, plus a public spectator map (your broadcast surface from day one).
Add the item economy (deploy AR bikes/decoys with wallets, Rungent pays to use, platform rake) — almost entirely existing components.
Add sponsored checkpoints, then scale prize with sponsorship.
Betting and real-human runners only later, each behind its own legal/safety gate.
